# Project 50K problem solution and market research

Research snapshot: **3 October 2026, Europe/Istanbul**. Prepared for the owner and subsequent development agents. This is public-source desk research, an evidence-gap assessment and a proposed validation design. No customer study, live detector backtest, paid-data test or commercial offer was executed.

**Real events and a substantial information stream are established. A recurring, inadequately served problem among the intended Turkish users—and a paid advantage for this particular product—are not yet established.** The most defensible proposition is to reduce the effort of understanding and verifying relevant changes in followed assets. Treat that as a solution hypothesis, not proven demand or fraud prediction.

## Evidence and its limits

| Question | Current conclusion | Basis and remaining gap |
|---|---|---|
| Does information arrive often? | Yes, at platform scale. | MKK's complete published 2025 KAP aggregate contains 161,759 notifications. It includes funds, issuers and regulators, routine reports and revisions; it is not the number of anomalies in a user's watchlist. |
| Can information become inconsistent or consequential? | Yes, observed cases exist. | SPK explicitly corrected conflicting public investor-count information on 23 September 2026. Official company amendments and an unusual-price-movement explanation provide different mechanisms. Selected cases establish presence, not incidence. |
| Are unusual price moves a recurring phenomenon? | Yes, in specified historical samples and methods. | Original US and crypto papers report frequent statistical detections. Different rules, sample periods and counting units prevent transfer to our catalog or current markets. |
| Do our users spend too much effort resolving them? | Unknown. | Investor attention is measured in original behavioral research; Turkish source-switching effort, missed-event burden and unmet needs were not measured here. |
| Does this solution beat existing tools? | Unknown; meaningful overlap is verified. | KAP already advertises following and instant notifications. Other official/vendor routes already cover account notifications, fund comparison, price alerts and Turkish AI research. No hands-on task comparison was executed. |
| Is it worth paying for? | Unknown. | Registry participation and incident exposure do not measure demand, conversion, retention or willingness to pay. No paying TAM, revenue projection or market-share assumption is supplied. |

## What frequency can honestly mean

A raw statistical crossing, a distinct event episode, an important development and a useful delivered alert answer different questions. An official amendment can matter without a price spike. A price spike can have ordinary context. A large chain transfer does not establish a sale, crime or harm. Several assets or several notifications can refer to one shared event.

| Measured quantity | Published observation | Denominator and interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| All KAP notifications in 2025 | **161,759** | Full calendar year, across the reported KAP population. Derived throughput: **443.18 per calendar day**, not per trading day, stock or user. |
| KAP special disclosures in 2025 | **35,944** | Same publication population/year; **98.48 per calendar day**. This category is not a count of harmful or unusual developments. |
| KAP fund notifications in 2025 | **79,357** | Fund publications in that aggregate; neither distinct fund problems nor daily fund-flow anomalies. |
| US minute-return threshold detections | Authors report **177,674**, approximately **7–8 per stock-day** | Joulin et al.: 149 trading days in 2005–2006, absolute minute return exceeding four times a 120-minute mean absolute return. The inspected description does not establish a one-sided window; text alternates between 163 and 166 stocks. Preserve the reported approximate rate rather than claim an independently reconciled estimate. This is not four standard deviations or our robust-return trigger. |
| BTC econometric jumps | **499 / 865 eligible test days**, or **57.69 jumps per 100 test days** | Saef et al., 12 April 2019–27 September 2021, pooled tick trades with method-specific preprocessing. Table 4 labels the numerator jumps; distinct jump-day probability was not independently reconciled. |
| ETH econometric jumps | **277 / 865**, or **32.02 jumps per 100 test days** | Same study/eligibility caveat. The six-coin total is 1,392 detections; no current user-alert frequency follows. |
| BIST circuit-breaker affected dates | **Not estimated** | Current official rules were checked; a complete annual activation listing was not recovered. From 1 September 2025 the index-wide rule changed to a single 6% decline threshold. Regimes and repeat activations must be separated in a future count. |
| Each of this project's 28 anomaly rules | **Not estimated** | No complete admitted historical universe was obtained and backtested. All 28 entries are explicitly mapped with null frequencies in the [frequency map](../research/problem_market/ANOMALY_FREQUENCY_MAP.json). Unknown is not zero. |

KAP's five published categories sum exactly to 161,759: 79,357 fund + 35,944 special + 16,920 regulatory + 15,426 other + 14,112 financial-report notifications. Two official layouts agree. Annual totals divided by 365 are averages of publication throughput; daily peaks and a specific watchlist's exposure remain unknown. Dividing this mixed population by a year-end listed-company count would be invalid.

The same MKK report states approximately **five million KAP quick searches annually**, averaging roughly **14,000 daily**. This is a reported usage signal for information seeking through an existing service, not five million distinct people, failed searches, unmet needs or demand for our product.

The crypto paper's table ratios are event-count/test-day ratios. Its introductory wording describes roughly 58%/32% of days, but this review does not promote that wording into a verified distinct-day probability. The US paper's approximate rate has its own denominator ambiguity. Both establish recurring method-specific detections in their samples, not harmful-event prevalence, causal accuracy or our thresholds' calibration.

Other original evidence includes estimated wash-volume shares, a preliminary vendor count of crypto hacks and a joint SEC/CFTC report on the 2010 market disruption. Their units are respectively trade value, identified incidents and executions inside one event. They are documented in the cross-market appendix and are not pooled into an anomaly percentage.

## Market scale and the customer who might have the problem

The official VAP screen dated **2 October 2026** reports approximately **6.62 million equity investors, 5.95 million fund investors and 3.72 million crypto investors**. These are overlapping participation cohorts, including different individual/institutional groupings. They must not be added into unique customers. The screen's separate monthly-active measures have unrecovered methodological details and are not used as paying-user estimates.

MKK added crypto-balance investors to a bulletin aggregate on **6 August 2026**. Its resulting monthly increase is not organic growth. The latest readable August bulletin, September system statistics and October screen contain scope/version differences which remain unreconciled. Their numbers are preserved separately; no growth curve is created by splicing them. A domestic US-equity-investor cohort was not established here. Neither foreign ownership nor a Turkish registry total substitutes for that missing segment.

**Proposed initial recruit:** a self-directed investor who repeatedly investigates actual changes in followed assets and consults more than one source. Recruitment should include satisfied incumbent users, quiet-period users and people who prefer fewer checks. A million-person registry category does not identify this behavior. The intersection of markets, frequency of the job and addressable paying cohort are all unknown. Selecting a first validation segment does not reduce the owner's four-market/on-chain target.

## The problem and the proposed solution

Candidate job: **“Something changed in an asset I follow. Help me establish what changed, when it became knowable, what supports it and what remains unresolved.”** The product would earn its place through better correct task completion, lower verification effort and tolerable attention/cost burden.

| Trigger and job | Existing alternative | Solution hypothesis | Falsifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| A company or fund publishes something relevant | KAP's free archive, following and instant mobile notifications | Join the dated disclosure to the relevant measured change and preserve revisions | KAP already resolves the user's question quickly; our explanation adds no useful context |
| A fund's valuation, category or published composition changes | Bank/broker, TEFAS and fund documents; exact user's entitlement matters | Explain comparable dated facts, separating valuation and publication time | Delayed inputs create false urgency or the existing route answers equally well |
| Price/volume deviates from recent comparable behavior | Broker/exchange alarms, charts and watchlists | Show raw change plus prior context and bounded source investigation | A simple alarm is sufficient, or added alerts cause more checking without better understanding |
| A statement is corrected or sources conflict | Original official documents and existing Turkish AI research | Make revision order, claim support and unresolved conflicts inspectable | Existing tools give an equally correct answer, or our summary raises confidence while reducing accuracy |
| A crypto move coincides with chain activity | Exchange charts, explorers and specialist on-chain tools | Tie finality-qualified observations to the proper window without ownership/sale assumptions | Attribution is too uncertain, stale or costly to improve the task |
| A user checks assets across markets | Their actual multi-tool routine, terminal, saved pages or deliberate infrequent checking | One watchlist with consistent evidence and uncertainty language | Cross-market tasks rarely co-occur or the unified view increases effort |

These hypotheses are conditional. “KAP alerts” alone is weak differentiation; sourced Turkish chat also has incumbent overlap. Our proposed advantage is consistent event-time evidence, measured context and honest abstention across the accepted markets. No observed superiority, defensibility or willingness to switch is claimed. [PROBLEM_SOLUTION_HYPOTHESES.json](../research/problem_market/PROBLEM_SOLUTION_HYPOTHESES.json) makes the evidence and disconfirmation conditions reusable.

## Measurement that could confirm or reject the proposition

The next useful preparation is a joint measurement specification, not a larger list of spectacular incidents. The following work is proposed, not executed or permission to contact users, acquire restricted data or charge money.

1. **Define exposure before counting.** Freeze each asset/source universe, interval, session/calendar, revision availability, field eligibility and numerical trigger where applicable. Numerical detectors need prior-only baselines; official access/correction/schedule facts need fixed event definitions. Record excluded and unavailable opportunities separately. Preserve ordinary periods as well as crises and delisted assets where relevant.
2. **Count distinct layers.** Report candidates per eligible interval and asset-day; distinct episodes per covered asset-period; reviewer-defined useful investigations; and actual notifications per user-week under a declared watchlist/cadence. Join related revisions and shared market episodes without concealing separate important events. Unlock schedules, their revisions and observed finalized transfers require separate counts. Do not infer user-week rates from platform totals.
3. **Review both flags and non-flags.** With independent, score-blinded labeling, distinguish deviation, sourced context, unknown cause, scheduled event, consequence and user relevance. Sample both flagged and unflagged opportunities to assess misses. Measure disagreement, abstention, missing data, review effort and retrieval cost. Choose sample precision after defining the target, especially for rare events; a handful of selected cases cannot establish sensitivity.
4. **Observe the last real user task.** Before showing a demo, reconstruct the person's recent incident and their actual route, including “nothing recently.” Separate recalled effort from observed timing and require public/redacted artifacts where feasible. Convenience interviews may reveal mechanisms, not population prevalence. Ask about alternatives they already trust and what problem remains unresolved.
5. **Compare correct completion against the next-best route.** Counterbalance matched tasks with independent answer keys and an explicit historical information cutoff. Include quiet periods, corrections, contradictions, stale data and a movement with no supported cause. Measure correct source-verified answers, effort, unsupported conclusions and source/time comprehension; preference and confidence alone are insufficient.
6. **Test ordinary use and payment separately.** A later consented pilot can assess voluntary return, mute behavior and useless repeated checks. A subsequently authorized priced offer or voluntary payment can test demand. Stated interest, waitlists and a crisis cohort are not purchase proof. Define worthwhile effort saved and acceptable error/alert burden for the actual job before seeing results.

If useful tasks are only episodic, investigate a digest or on-demand investigation proposition rather than assuming daily notification retention. If incumbent tools already solve them, revise the value proposition. If accuracy does not improve with confidence, revise explanation behavior. If value exists but payment does not, revisit economics with the owner; do not silently change the market target or invent revenue.
