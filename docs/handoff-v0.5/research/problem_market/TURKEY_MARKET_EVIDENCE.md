# Turkish investor-market evidence and first-job hypothesis

Research date: **2026-10-03**. Official sources were read selectively through public document retrieval; no accounts, user contact, purchases or live provider tests. The owner's four-market target—BIST, funds, crypto and US equities with meaningful on-chain experience—remains unchanged.

**There is a substantial relevant investor population. These statistics do not establish how many people experience the proposed problem, how often they need help, or whether they will pay.** They justify investigating a problem among existing investors, not announcing a paying TAM.

## Crypto coverage and overlap are consequential

MKK's **16 July 2026** launch announcement describes statistics built from KVHS reporting. It distinguishes approximately **5.6 million investors who had ever traded** from approximately **3.2 million investors then holding a balance**; about **4.8 million were already registered at MKK** [TR-S06]. Those are different cohorts. Existing MKK registration demonstrates potential overlap with the registry, not proof that the same people currently own equities or funds. Provider membership/reporting coverage can change.

Do not add equity, fund and crypto investor counts into unique Turkish customers. Do not add individuals and institutional accounts to a retail TAM, treat holders as active users, treat accounts as people, or infer four-market ownership from registry participation. A dated domestic US-equity investor cohort was not established in this review. Foreign share ownership, domestic stock-market users and crypto holders are not substitutes for that missing cohort.

## An observable problem episode, with a bounded inference

SPK's **23 September 2026** statement explicitly responds to conflicting public reports about affected investor counts. It identifies **455,758 unique investors** across **131 funds belonging to seven portfolio-management companies**, following the **17 September** liquidation decisions [TR-S07]. This is concrete primary evidence that a consequential event produced conflicting information requiring official clarification.

It supports testing whether users need fast, source-backed event interpretation. It does **not** show that the project could have predicted the event, quantify investor losses, establish the normal frequency of anomalies, or establish a recurring subscription market. This affected cohort must not be used as a paying customer estimate or added to general fund-investor totals.

## Plausible first segment and job—hypotheses to test

**Candidate first segment:** self-directed Turkish individual investors who already monitor a personal watchlist across more than one asset domain and repeatedly consult official disclosures plus market data. The official equity/fund counts suggest a sizable pool in which such users might exist; the intersection, workflow burden and willingness to pay remain unknown. A candidate recruit is defined by observed behavior, not membership in a million-person registry.

**First job:** “Something changed in an asset I follow. Tell me what changed, when it became knowable, which official evidence supports it, and what remains uncertain, so I can decide what to investigate.” This applies to the existing four-market/on-chain target. It is an investigation workflow, without a promised buy/sell instruction or fraud verdict.

The proposed solution should earn its place by reducing effort to verify a specific event, distinguishing published facts from mathematical estimates, linking the correct source/date and admitting unsupported causal explanations. The customer outcome to test is faster, more accurate understanding with tolerable alert burden—not simply more generated alerts. This is an inference from the evidence and product contracts, not an observed customer result.

| Research question | Cheapest honest evidence to collect later | What would weaken the hypothesis |
|---|---|---|
| Does the job recur for the same user? | Recent-event walkthrough and a short diary of actual verification tasks, including no-event days | Rare use confined to a crisis or a one-time pitch example |
| Is current verification burdensome? | Observe the user's existing source/tool sequence and time/error points | Existing broker/fund app solves it in a few steps |
| Does our evidence view improve understanding? | Compare a specific artifact against their usual workflow; score fact/date/source correctness | Faster answer but greater unsupported certainty or no time saved |
| Is it a paying problem? | Test an explicit priced offer with the relevant user cohort after permission; distinguish stated interest from commitment | Praise without willingness to switch, return or pay |

These are proposals, not completed interviews, outreach or pricing tests. No contact occurred. Market size does not answer them.

## How to use these numbers in the project

An honest market statement is: **“Official October screens show millions of equity, fund and crypto participants; a recent SPK clarification shows a real event-information problem. We are testing a recurring verification job among existing investors.”** A claim that all those investors need anomaly alerts, that assets under custody equal software revenue opportunity, or that a fixed percentage will pay is not supported.

Market assets, institutions, registered users, balances, active accounts and paid software users belong to different units. No paying TAM/SAM/SOM or annual revenue calculation is made. The four-market/on-chain demonstration target is not narrowed by selecting a candidate first customer segment. Empirical event frequency must be separately measured with fixed definitions, universes and exposure denominators; these population reports provide none of those rates.

Exact source URLs, publication/observation dates, selected page/line depth, web references and unresolved inconsistencies are recorded in `turkey_market_sources.json`. No full PDF reading, comprehensive national crypto coverage or live customer-demand validation is implied.
