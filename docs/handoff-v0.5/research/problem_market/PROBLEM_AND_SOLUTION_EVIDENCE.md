# Problem and solution evidence

Research snapshot: 2026-10-03 UTC. This is desk research and a proposed validation protocol. No investors were contacted, observed, interviewed, enrolled or charged. No product-market fit, market prevalence, comprehension improvement or willingness to pay is established. The accepted BIST, Turkish fund, US equity and crypto/on-chain breadth remains unchanged.

## What the evidence currently supports

Investor attention is a real behavior with meaningful variation; disclosure presentation can affect judgment. These findings support investigating the problem. They do **not** establish that Turkish investors regularly suffer a particular unsolved monitoring problem, that extra alerts help, or that an AI explanation is worth paying for. The most defensible product hypothesis is narrower than “investors need more information”: **some investors may need less effort to identify a relevant change, verify what is known at that time, and decide whether further investigation is necessary.** This is a hypothesis about a job, not a newly approved scope restriction.

| Evidence | Observation and method | What it supports / does not support |
|---|---|---|
| PM-S01 — Sicherman, Loewenstein, Seppi & Utkus, *Financial Attention*, RFS 29(4), 2016 | US Vanguard records for 2007–2008 covered 1,168,309 retirement-account investors; main analysis randomly selected 100,000 paperless accounts. Daily login/trading records were analyzed with aggregate and individual panel regressions. Median days with logins were 36 versus one trading day over the two years. In a basic aggregate specification controlling for weekday, the authors estimate 9.5% fewer logins following a negative Dow day; behavior changes around extreme declines. | Monitoring is distinct from trading and attention varies with conditions. Observational associations and logins as an attention proxy do not measure source-switching burden, Turkish demand, missed-event harm, or the benefit of increasing attention. Retirement accounts principally in funds are not a representative sample of today's four-market Turkish audience. Main paper read; supplementary Internet Appendix not reviewed. |
| PM-S02 — Rennekamp, author discussion of *Processing Fluency and Investors’ Reactions to Disclosure Readability*, 2012 | The author describes a controlled experiment holding disclosure length/content constant: readable presentation amplified reactions to good and bad news and perceived reliance, without a significant difference in information gathered. Making readability variation explicit reduced the initial effect. | Presentation changes can alter judgment beyond understanding. Clear Turkish summaries need comprehension and calibration tests, not just preference ratings. This author summary was read in full, but the journal full-text request redirected to its abstract. The study's denominator, collection period and numerical effect size were **not verified**, so it is not treated as a fully appraised quantitative study. |

These are investor-relevant behavioral sources; clinical alarm-fatigue evidence is not used as a proxy for retail finance. Direct evidence of the magnitude of information overload in the intended Turkish segment remains missing. “Attention is limited,” “people check their investments,” and “people dislike complexity” are different claims from “our users cannot adequately monitor their assets using their current tools.”

The following remain untested: incident frequency; actual time lost across sources; the cost of delayed understanding; whether investors deliberately avoid notifications; whether a source-linked investigation saves enough effort to matter; whether users distinguish temporal coincidence from cause; whether they return during ordinary weeks; and willingness to pay at an economically viable price. Current vendor pages establish existing offers. They do not establish unmet pain or dissatisfaction.

## Practical validation design — proposed, not executed

### 1. Reconstruct the last real incident before showing the product

Ask in natural Turkish: “Takip ettiğiniz bir yatırım hakkında en son ne zaman bir şey öğrenmeye çalıştınız? O gün ne oldu?” Follow the person's actual example, including “nothing recently.” Obtain an approximate date, asset and trigger; ask how they first learned it and what they did next. With consent, use a public disclosure/link or a redacted artifact to anchor recall. Do not collect holdings quantities, account credentials or private balances for this study.

Record the original question, first source, sequence of tools, estimated active effort versus elapsed waiting, interruptions, language difficulty, confidence before/after and whether the question was resolved. Separate recalled estimates from observed timings. Ask what they stopped doing, what they already pay for, and what they would use if this product did not exist. Ask whether the issue warranted any further research at all; do not infer material harm from a subsequent price fall or a memorable news story. “Would you like AI alerts?” and a polished demo's positive reaction are weak evidence.

### 2. Sample for disconfirmation across the accepted breadth

Recruit only through a subsequently authorized channel. Include users with different experience, monitoring cadence, market combinations, mobile/desktop habits, paid/free tools and notification preferences. Include satisfied KAP/broker/Evo users, fund investors who rarely trade, crypto users who distrust automated wallet labels, people who muted alerts, and people who intentionally avoid frequent checking. Neither exclusion of a market nor a fixed six interviews is a proof rule. A purposive convenience sample can reveal mechanisms and counterexamples; it cannot estimate population prevalence.

Maintain a case matrix: market/job, actual incident, evidence available then, existing alternative, unresolved friction, desired cadence and negative case. Continue only while additional cases can resolve a live decision or an underrepresented counterexample. Report recruitment source, refusals, missing strata and how researcher prompting could have shaped responses. If prevalence estimates become decision-critical, specify the target population, sampling frame and precision first rather than extrapolating interview counts.

### 3. Compare task behavior, with independent answer keys

After identifying real jobs, propose a counterbalanced comparison using different but matched historical/public cases. Compare the participant's existing route with the product prototype. Freeze inputs at an explicit availability cutoff to prevent hindsight information; include ordinary/no-material-event cases, stale sources, conflicting evidence and a disclosed fact without a known causal explanation. Keep first exposure and practice effects visible.

Measure time to a **correct, source-verified answer**, source switches, unanswered questions, unsupported conclusions and whether the participant can identify observation time, publication time, limitations and the distinction between fact and possible explanation. Ask them to locate the underlying document. An evaluator who did not write the explanation should score the answer against public evidence. Confidence and perceived usefulness are secondary; higher confidence with unchanged or worse correctness is a failure mode. No return/profit metric or “losses prevented” claim follows from this exercise.

### 4. Check ordinary-use value and payment separately

If a prototype clears comprehension and evidence checks, a bounded, consented pilot can compare chosen notification cadence and active monitoring effort over both quiet and eventful periods. Log repeated opens that resolve nothing, mute/disable behavior, redundant alerts, source-verification actions and voluntary return for actual work. Record harmful confusion as well as success. The current offline labs measure constructed cases; they supply neither real alert-fatigue nor human trust/retention results.

Use a later explicitly authorized commercial test to distinguish survey intent from behavior: a clearly described offer and price, a participant choosing it over their current alternative, and eventually voluntary actual payment with transparent terms. Do not use deceptive checkout, fabricated scarcity, or an unpaid waitlist as payment proof. No such offer or collection has occurred.

## Decision rules to agree before collecting results

Use contextual decision criteria, not invented universal conversion/interview thresholds. Evidence for continuing a workflow would be repeated, artifact-backed unresolved incidents plus better correct task performance than the next-best route and voluntary ordinary-use value, including counterexamples. Specify the minimum worthwhile saved effort and acceptable error/attention burden for the actual job and price **before** the comparison; make owner approval of those criteria explicit.

If users already resolve incidents easily, reconsider that workflow's positioning. If only major crises create demand, test an episodic investigation/digest proposition before assuming daily alert retention. If summaries raise confidence without source understanding, revise explanation and uncertainty presentation before broadening claims. If value exists but payment does not, revisit economics and distribution with the owner; do not silently change the four-market target. If repeated tasks produce unsupported causal/fraud conclusions, pause that explanation behavior even if users say they like it.
