## 2. Narrow differentiation hypothesis
A Turkish fund/BIST investigation experience that preserves as-of dates, separates underlying exposure from current holdings, explains market movement with official evidence and shows when data is insufficient. Cross-market search is supporting convenience. Design quality and readable Turkish are adoption hypotheses, not a defensible moat by themselves.

The above capabilities are publisher descriptions checked on 2026-10-02, not independently measured product outcomes. Fintables' estimated fund-price feature is an estimate, not observed official intraday NAV. Do not import competitor accuracy, wallet-count or instant-delivery marketing as evidence of measured performance. Recheck dated product pages before pitching.

## 3. Comparison protocol
Use the same five user jobs across products: find a fund, understand an unusual return, inspect official sources, configure low-noise monitoring and ask an event-specific follow-up. Record date, account tier, screen evidence, response time, data delays, citations and limitations. Use lawful screenshots internally; do not redistribute copyrighted product assets without permission.

Score only observed task outcomes, not presumed product quality. Track “not tested” separately from “absent.” Ask users which current alternative they would stop using and why; “all in one” may not offset trusted incumbent workflows.

## 4. Defensibility and risks
Potential durable assets: high-quality timestamped evidence, reviewed Turkish event taxonomy, evaluated low-noise alerting and accepted distribution/data relationships. Risks: incumbents add similar AI, data costs exceed subscription revenue, excessive alerts erode trust, or fund historical fields are inaccessible. Proposed mitigations are testable experiments, not evidence these risks are solved.
